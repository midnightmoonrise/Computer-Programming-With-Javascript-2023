Alpine skiing is a winter sport involving, according to Ben Juo, "speeding down the mountain at 40 miles per hour on two slices of tree." It involves skiing down a course (correctly) - determined by gates - red or blue plastic poles/gates - as fast as possible. It is much better than nordic skiing, which is pretty much track on skis. I enjoy alpine skiing for the stress relief it gives when you slam the first slalom gate down, and for the lovely purple bruises you get when you whack GS gates down, even if you have protective equipment on. Also, though you race individually, you train with a team, and that tends to be really fun.

There are 4 main disciplines for alpine skiing, going from shortest ski length to longest: Slalom (the one with the pole-like gates), Giant Slalom, Super-G, and Downhill (with the jumps!). Click the image below to see a course for each.

A slalom course

Equipment

  • Race skis for every discipline you race in, of the right length
  • Poles - straight ones for slalom, bent for anything else
  • A race suit if you're racing
  • Goggles
  • Properly fitted ski boots with the appropriate flex
  • Gloves
  • An FIS approved hard-eared helmet (can get a separate soft-eared one for slalom)
  • Gate guards for slalom
  • Shin guards for slalom
  • A chin bar for slalom; make sure it fits your helmet
